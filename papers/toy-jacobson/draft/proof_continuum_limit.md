# The continuum statement behind anchor 1, and the rate of its lattice approximation

`papers/toy-jacobson`, Layer 7. Companion to `MANIFEST.md` (anchors 1, 5, 8, 9) and to
`vacuum/geometry/jacobson_continuum.py` / `tests/test_toy_jacobson_continuum.py` /
`data/H_continuum_check.json`, which carry the numbers quoted below.

The lattice measurement of anchor 1 is: for the massless nearest-neighbour chain, the
residual

  r(K) = max over (ball B, variation v) of |δS_v(B) − δ⟨K_loc⟩_v(B)| / |δS_v(B)|

falls from 9.09e-02 (L = 4) to 3.4e-04 (L = 48) with a fitted exponent −2.27, and the two
continuum extrapolations give r_∞ = −1.9e-04 (1/L² fit) and +4.6e-04 (fitted power). This
document states and proves the **continuum** statement that r(K) is approximating, and
derives the **rate** at which the lattice approximates it, separating what is proved, what
is assumed, and what is only observed.

## 0. Notation and the objects the lattice uses

**Continuum.** A = (−R, R) ⊂ ℝ at t = 0 in 1+1 Minkowski space. β(x) = (R² − x²)/(2R) is the
CHM conformal-Killing weight; the vector field ξ = 2πβ(x)∂_x is the boost-like conformal
Killing field that preserves the causal diamond of A. The modular coordinate is

  z(x) = ln((R + x)/(R − x)),  dz/dx = 1/β(x),  x(z) = R tanh(z/2),  (0.1)

in which the flow of ξ is the translation z ↦ z + 2πt.

**Lattice.** H = ½(p·p + xᵀKx) on N sites with Dirichlet walls, K the massless
nearest-neighbour Laplacian (`coupling_chain_K(N, [1.0])`), lattice spacing a = 1. The ball
B is a centred interval of L = 2R sites, with site centres x_i ∈ {−R+½, …, R−½} and
boundaries at ±R. The local energy density is the PSD **bond split**
(`local_energy_forms(K, split='bond')`)

  h_i = p_i²/2 + M_i x_i²/2 + ¼ Σ_j c_ij (x_i − x_j)²,  c_ij = −K_ij, M_i = Σ_j K_ij, (0.2)

and Σ_i h_i = H exactly. For the massless chain M_i = 0 at every bulk site (the rows of the
Dirichlet Laplacian sum to zero away from the walls), so on a centred ball the on-site term
of (0.2) is absent and h_i is kinetic + half of each adjacent bond. The lattice ansatz is

  K_loc = 2π Σ_{i∈B} β_i h_i,  β_i = (R² − x_i²)/(2R)  ("site-centred", `beta_at='site'`), (0.3)

or, equivalently to O(a²), each **bond** counted once with β at its midpoint
("bond-centred", `beta_at='bond'`). The variations are single-mode squeezes of a normal mode
u of K, x → e^ε x, p → e^{−ε} p along u, whose first-order covariance change is
δV = ε·diag(uuᵀ/ω, −ω uuᵀ) (`squeeze_variation`), restricted to reflection-**odd** u.

## 1. The entanglement first law as an exact identity

**Proposition 1.1 (first law).** Let ρ(ε) be a family of density operators on a Hilbert
space, differentiable at ε = 0 in the sense R1–R3 below, with ρ ≡ ρ(0) and
K ≡ −ln ρ (the modular Hamiltonian, defined up to an additive constant). Then

  dS/dε |₀ = d⟨K⟩/dε |₀,  S(ε) = −Tr ρ(ε) ln ρ(ε),  ⟨K⟩(ε) = Tr ρ(ε) K.  (1.1)

*Proof.* Write δρ = dρ/dε|₀. Differentiating S:

  dS/dε = −Tr[ δρ ln ρ ] − Tr[ ρ · d(ln ρ)/dε ].  (1.2)

The second term vanishes: for any differentiable positive family,
Tr[ρ d(ln ρ)/dε] = Tr[δρ] — use the integral representation
ln ρ = ∫₀^∞ ds ( (1+s)⁻¹ − (ρ+s)⁻¹ ), whence d(ln ρ)/dε = ∫₀^∞ ds (ρ+s)⁻¹ δρ (ρ+s)⁻¹, and
by cyclicity Tr[ρ (ρ+s)⁻¹ δρ (ρ+s)⁻¹] = Tr[δρ ρ (ρ+s)⁻²]; integrating
∫₀^∞ ρ(ρ+s)⁻² ds = 1 gives Tr[ρ d(ln ρ)/dε] = Tr[δρ] = 0, because Tr ρ(ε) = 1 for all ε.
Hence dS/dε = −Tr[δρ ln ρ] = Tr[δρ K] = d⟨K⟩/dε. ∎

**Regularity actually needed (R1–R3).** (R1) ρ(ε) is trace class, positive, and
ε ↦ ρ(ε) is differentiable in trace norm at ε = 0, so Tr δρ = 0. (R2) ρ has no kernel on
the support of δρ, so ln ρ is defined where it is used; equivalently the spectrum of ρ is
bounded away from 0 on the relevant subspace, or δρ is supported where ρ > 0. (R3)
Tr[δρ K] converges absolutely. In field theory (R1)–(R3) all fail as stated for a sharp
region — S is UV divergent and K is unbounded — and the standard resolution is that the
**difference** of the two sides is what is finite: rigorously, one states the first law as
the vanishing of the first-order relative entropy,
S(ρ(ε)‖ρ) = Δ⟨K⟩ − ΔS = O(ε²) ≥ 0, which is the form used by
Blanco–Casini–Hung–Myers (JHEP 08 (2013) 060, Sec. 2) and which requires only that
ε ↦ S(ρ(ε)‖ρ) be differentiable at 0 — where it attains its minimum 0, hence has vanishing
first derivative. **Everything in this document uses the first law in that relative-entropy
form.**

Two consequences used later:

* **(1.a)** For a Gaussian state with covariance V and Williamson eigenvalues ν_k,
  S = Σ_k [(ν_k+½)ln(ν_k+½) − (ν_k−½)ln(ν_k−½)] and (1.1) reads
  δS = ½ Tr[G δV] with G the quadratic form of K; in the Williamson frame
  G is diagonal with entries g(ν_k) = ln((ν_k+½)/(ν_k−½)). This is
  `_mode_contributions` / `_g_of_nu` in `vacuum/geometry/jacobson.py`, and
  `first_law_audit` verifies it by independent routes at ≤ 8.6e-09 (anchor 2).
* **(1.b)** If the variation is generated by a unitary **belonging to the algebra of A**,
  then ρ_A(ε) = UρU† has S(ε) = S(0), so δS = 0 and therefore δ⟨K_A⟩ = 0 as well. This is
  the basis of the shape test of §5.2.

Proposition 1.1 is an identity: it holds for *every* dynamics and *every* first-order
variation, with K the true modular Hamiltonian. It carries no information about locality.
The content of the toy is entirely in §2 — whether K is the **local** integral.

## 2. The modular Hamiltonian of an interval, and which field the theorem is about

### 2.1 The theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (Bisognano–Wichmann; Hislop–Longo; Casini–Huerta–Myers).** Let 𝒜(O) be the
local algebra of a chiral conformal net on the line with stress tensor T (central charge c),
in its vacuum state. For the interval A = (−R, R) the modular Hamiltonian is the local
integral of the conformal-Killing energy:

  K_A = 2π ∫_{−R}^{R} β(x) T₀₀(x) dx + const,  β(x) = (R² − x²)/(2R),  (2.1)

and the modular flow is the geometric Möbius flow of ξ = 2πβ ∂_x, i.e. z ↦ z + 2πt in the
coordinate (0.1).

*References, precisely.* Bisognano–Wichmann (J. Math. Phys. 16 (1975) 985; 17 (1976) 303)
prove the wedge case: for a Rindler wedge the modular group of the vacuum is the boost, so
K_wedge = 2π∫ x T₀₀(x) dx. Hislop–Longo (Commun. Math. Phys. 84 (1982) 71) extend this to
double cones for the free massless scalar field — conformal covariance maps the wedge to the
double cone, and the modular group is the corresponding conformal flow; in 1+1 the chiral
(derivative) field is the relevant local field and the double cone of A is the diamond over
A. Casini–Huerta–Myers (JHEP 05 (2011) 036, Sec. 2) give the same statement in the form
(2.1) for a sphere of radius R in a CFT in any d — the conformal map from the sphere's
causal diamond to Rindler space — and it is their normalisation and weight that the lattice
ansatz (0.3) discretises. **This document does not reprove Theorem 2.1; it is the one
imported input.**

### 2.2 The zero-mode / IR subtlety of the 1+1 massless scalar, stated precisely

Theorem 2.1 holds for the algebra generated by the **chiral current** J = ∂φ (equivalently,
by the derivative field, equivalently by the stress tensor and the current net). It does
**not** hold for the algebra generated by the 1+1 massless scalar φ itself, for two related
reasons:

1. φ is not a well-defined operator-valued distribution in 1+1: its two-point function
   ⟨φ(x)φ(y)⟩ = −(1/4π) ln[μ²(x−y)²] carries an IR scale μ and the massless 1+1 scalar has
   no vacuum in the usual sense (Coleman's theorem). Only ∂φ, and exponentials at zero
   total charge, are IR-safe.
2. Consequently the reduced state of the interval for the φ-algebra carries an extra
   contribution from the **zero mode** φ̄_A = (1/|A|)∫_A φ, whose conjugate momentum is the
   total momentum in A. Casini–Huerta (J. Phys. A 42 (2009) 504007) discuss exactly this:
   the entropy of an interval for the 2d massless scalar carries, beyond the universal
   (c/3) ln(ℓ/ε), an IR/zero-mode term, and the modular Hamiltonian of the φ-algebra is not
   the purely local (2.1).

**This is not a technicality for the lattice model; it is measured.** On the Dirichlet chain
the ball's top Williamson eigenvalue is the lattice avatar of φ̄_A (ν_top = 1.205, 1.218 at
L = 16, 32, growing logarithmically with N; `data/A_massless_chain.npz`,
`profile_L*_nu`), and anchor 1 records that reflection-**even** variations — which do
excite it — have r = 3.07, 2.83, 2.15 at L = 8, 16, 32: **O(1) and not decaying**, exactly
as Theorem 2.1 not applying to that sector requires.

**Lemma 2.2 (the odd sector is exactly blind to the zero mode).** Let the chain and the ball
be symmetric about the chain centre, and let u be a reflection-odd normal mode of K
(u_{N−1−i} = −u_i). Then the squeeze variation δV = ε·diag(uuᵀ/ω, −ω uuᵀ) restricted to B
has zero overlap with every reflection-**even** Williamson mode of the ball's vacuum
covariance V_B — in particular with the top mode φ̄_A.

*Proof.* V_B commutes with the reflection R of the ball, so its Williamson modes can be (and
in `williamson` are, up to degeneracy) chosen with definite parity; the diagonal entries of
W = S⁻¹ δV S⁻ᵀ that enter δS = ½ Σ_k g(ν_k) (W_kk + W_{L+k,L+k}) are, for a rank-one
δV built from u, proportional to (rowᵀ_k u)², and (rowᵀ_k u) = 0 whenever row_k is even and
u is odd. ∎

The same holds for the two-mode (odd × odd) squeezes of anchor 9 (`pair_variation`), since
W_kk = 2(row_k·u_a)(row_k·u_b). Numerically the ball's top-mode share of δS is 1.5e-17 over
every odd variation of anchor 1 (`odd_top_mode_share_max`).

**Therefore:** the quantity anchor 1 measures is a statement about the **current/derivative
sector** of the lattice scalar — precisely the sector in which Theorem 2.1 holds without the
IR caveat. The even sector is a different question, is measured to be O(1), and is
explained by the zero mode; anchor 8's projection removes the O(1) obstruction but leaves a
percent-level near-cancellation (MANIFEST, Limitations). Nothing below applies to the even
sector.

### 2.3 What the Dirichlet chain and the PSD bond split have to do with (2.1)

Three separate identifications are being made, and each is an assumption of a different
strength:

* **A1 (universality of the continuum limit).** The massless nearest-neighbour chain at
  wavelengths ≫ a is in the universality class of the c = 1 free boson, with v = 1 and
  ω(k) = 2 sin(k/2) = k(1 − k²/24 + …). Standard; not proved here. The Dirichlet walls make
  K positive definite (so the lattice has no exact zero mode) and put the chain on a finite
  interval; the ball is centred and L ≪ N, so the walls enter only through A5.
* **A2 (energy density).** The PSD bond split (0.2) is *a* lattice representative of T₀₀:
  Σ_i h_i = H exactly, and h_i → T₀₀(x_i)·a as a → 0 for smooth configurations. Different
  splits differ by a discrete divergence (the site-symmetrised split `split='site'` is
  another), i.e. by a total derivative, which is exactly the ambiguity that the weight β
  resolves at O(a²) — this is the whole subject of §4. The bond split is chosen because it
  is PSD piecewise, which is what makes a nonnegative weight meaningful in anchor 9.
* **A3 (the weight).** β_i in (0.3) is β evaluated at the site centres with the ball
  boundaries placed at ±R = ±L/2 (midway between the edge site and its outside neighbour).
  Any other placement of the boundary within one lattice spacing changes the answer at
  O(a/R) in the weight near the edge and is part of what §4 quantifies.

## 3. The continuum residual vanishes identically; the free weight must return β

### 3.1 The residual is identically zero in the continuum

**Corollary 3.1.** Let A = (−R, R) in the vacuum of a 1+1 chiral CFT and let ρ_A(ε) be any
differentiable family of states with ρ_A(0) the vacuum reduction. Then

  δS = δ⟨K_A⟩ = 2π ∫_{−R}^{R} β(x) δ⟨T₀₀(x)⟩ dx exactly, i.e. ρ ≡ (δS − δ⟨K_loc⟩)/δS = 0,
  (3.1)

for **every** such family — in particular for every squeeze, every coherent displacement,
every multi-mode Gaussian variation, and every non-Gaussian one.

*Proof.* Proposition 1.1 gives δS = δ⟨K_A⟩ (in the relative-entropy form); Theorem 2.1
identifies K_A with the local integral, whose first-order expectation is the right-hand
side because the additive constant drops out of a first-order variation of a normalised
state. ∎

So the continuum residual is not small — it is zero, identically, with no limit taken. **The
entire content of the lattice number r(K) is the *discretisation*.** This is the sense in
which "r → 0 for the wave chain" is a consistency check on CHM and not a discovery: it must
hold, and the only question is how fast. §4 answers that question.

### 3.2 The free-weight minimiser

**Corollary 3.2.** In the continuum, let 𝒲 be any set of measurable weights w ≥ 0 on A
containing β, and let the variation family 𝒱 be rich enough that

  ∫_A (w(x) − β(x)) δ⟨T₀₀(x)⟩ dx = 0 for all δ ∈ 𝒱  ⟹  w = β a.e.  (3.2)

Then β is the unique minimiser of sup_{δ∈𝒱} |δS − 2π∫ w δ⟨T₀₀⟩| / |δS| over 𝒲, with value 0.

*Proof.* By Cor. 3.1 the value at w = β is 0, which is the minimum of a nonnegative
functional; uniqueness is the hypothesis (3.2). ∎

The hypothesis (3.2) is a **completeness** assumption on the variation family: it says the
family's energy-density profiles {δ⟨T₀₀⟩} span a set whose annihilator in L¹(A) is trivial.
It is *false* for the family actually used.

### 3.3 What "found from nothing" proves, and what it does not

Anchor 9 minimises the residual over a three-mode nonnegative weight family and lands on the
CHM parabola (coefficients (1.0010, −1.0007, −0.0003)·πR/2, overlap ≥ 0.9992, normalisation
within 0.1 %, r_free = 4.1e-05, 1.2e-05, 2.5e-06 at L = 8, 16, 32). Given Cor. 3.2 the honest
reading is:

* **Proved (given §1–§2 and the measurement):** among the weights of the stated family, the
  minimiser of the lattice residual agrees with the CHM parabola to 0.1 % in shape and
  normalisation, and drives the residual 20–130× below what the CHM *discretisation* itself
  achieves. Since the continuum minimiser is β exactly (Cor. 3.2) whenever (3.2) holds, this
  is the expected result and is a quantitative check that the lattice family is close enough
  to the continuum that the continuum minimiser is visible.
* **Not proved:** that β is *singled out* by the data. Hypothesis (3.2) fails for this
  family: the MANIFEST records the variation family's numerical rank as 4, 6, 7–8 at
  10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁸ — i.e. the long-wavelength regime imposes only 5–6 independent conditions
  per ball — so any weight family with that many free parameters fits, and a per-site weight
  (L/2 + 1 parameters) reaches 2.1e-06 for the wave chain *and* 4.5e-05 for Lifshitz. The
  identification is therefore a statement at a fixed parameter count **below** the rank, and
  the parsimony gap (3 modes vs ≥ 11–12 for z = 2) is the claim, exactly as the MANIFEST
  states it.
* **Not proved, and not even true:** that the test measures a velocity. δS and δ⟨K⟩ are both
  properties of the *state*, and the vacuum of v²K is the vacuum of K; a free weight absorbs
  v exactly (anchor 9 measures this: r_free(v²K) = r_free(K) to machine precision). §4 below
  concerns only the massless v = 1 chain, and says nothing about v either.

## 4. The lattice rate

The continuum residual is exactly zero (Cor. 3.1). The lattice number is therefore a pure
discretisation error, and there are two independent sources: **where the weight β is
sampled** (a property of the ansatz) and **how far the lattice energy density and covariance
are from their continuum limits** (a property of the model). The first is computed exactly
below; the second is not, and that is the honest boundary of this section.

### 4.1 The weight-placement difference is exact algebra

**Proposition 4.1 (trapezoid minus midpoint, exact).** On the massless chain with a = 1 and
a centred ball B of L = 2R sites, write the bond energy O_b = ½ c (x_i − x_j)² of the bond
b = (i, j = i+1) with midpoint x_m = (x_i + x_j)/2. Both discretisations give the kinetic
piece p_i²/2 the weight β_i, and the on-site piece of (0.2) is absent (M_i = 0). They differ
only in the bond weights, and

* for a bond with both ends in B: w_site(b) − w_bond(b) = (β_i + β_j)/2 − β(x_m)
  = (a²/8) β''(x_m) = −a²/(8R) **exactly**, because β is a quadratic polynomial and its
  second-order Taylor expansion at x_m is exact;
* for each of the two bonds straddling ∂B: w_site(b) = β_edge/2 with
  β_edge = β(±(R − a/2)) = a/2 − a²/(8R), and w_bond(b) = β(±R) = 0, since the midpoint of a
  nearest-neighbour straddling bond sits exactly on the entangling point.

Hence, as operators,

  K_site − K_bond = 2π [ −(a²/8R) Σ_{b interior} O_b + (β_edge/2)(O_left + O_right) ]. (4.1)

There is no O(a⁴) remainder in (4.1): every step is an identity. ∎

**Proposition 4.2 (the variation, exact).** Let u be the exact lattice normal mode
u_i = A sin(k x_i) with ω = 2 sin(ka/2), and δV = ε diag(uuᵀ/ω, −ω uuᵀ) the first-order
squeeze. Then, with a = 1 and c = 1,

  δ⟨O_b⟩ = (ε ω A²/2) cos²(k x_m),   δ⟨p_i²/2⟩ = −(ε ω A²/2) sin²(k x_i). (4.2)

*Proof.* δ⟨(x_i − x_j)²⟩ = (ε/ω)(u_i − u_j)², and
(u_i − u_j)² = 4 sin²(k/2) A² cos²(k x_m) = ω² A² cos²(k x_m) by the product formula; and
δ⟨p_i²⟩ = −ε ω u_i². ∎

Combining (4.1) and (4.2), and dropping the common factor 2π ε ω A²/2:

  ΔdK ≡ dK_site − dK_bond ∝ −(1/8R) Σ_{m} cos²(k x_m) + β_edge cos²(kR),   (4.3)
  dK_bond ∝ −Σ_{i∈B} β_i sin²(k x_i) + Σ_{m} β(x_m) cos²(k x_m),           (4.4)

where i runs over the L site centres x_i = −R + ½ + i and m over the L − 1 interior bond
midpoints x_m = −R + 1, …, R − 1. Both are closed-form finite sums with no free parameter.

### 4.2 The exact lattice identity, and its numerical certificate

**Proposition 4.3.** With ρ_X ≡ (δS − δ⟨K_X⟩)/δS for X ∈ {site, bond} on the same ball and
the same variation,

  ρ_site − ρ_bond = −(1 − ρ_bond) · ΔdK / dK_bond,  (4.5)

with ΔdK and dK_bond the sums (4.3)–(4.4). *Proof.* δS = dK_bond/(1 − ρ_bond) by the
definition of ρ_bond, and ρ_site − ρ_bond = −(dK_site − dK_bond)/δS. ∎

(4.5) involves no approximation whatever: no continuum limit, no expansion, no fit. It is
implemented as `bond_split_discretization_prediction(L, wavelength, rho_bond=...)["A_exact"]`
(times 1/L²). **Certificate** (`data/H_continuum_check.json`, `H3_bond_split_prediction`,
N = 2400, L = 8, 16, 32 × three wavelengths): the maximum relative deviation of (4.5) from
the measured ρ_site − ρ_bond is **6.3e-12** — machine precision. Pinned by
`tests/test_toy_jacobson_continuum.py::test_bond_split_prediction_reproduces_the_lattice_residual`
at 1e-8.

### 4.3 The continuum limit of the coefficient

**Corollary 4.4 (leading order).** Replace the sums in (4.3)–(4.4) by integrals over
(−R, R) and ω by k, and set κ = kR = πL/λ. Then

  ρ_site − ρ_bond = A(κ)/L² · (1 + O(1/L)),
  A(κ) = −4 C(κ)/D(κ),
  C(κ) = −(1 + sin 2κ/(2κ))/8 + cos²κ / 2,
  D(κ) = −cos 2κ/(2κ²) + sin 2κ/(4κ³).   (4.6)

In particular A(0⁺) = −3/2 (the two terms of C are −1/4 and +1/2, and D(0⁺) = 2/3), and
A(π/4) = −0.3521, A(π/8) = −1.2083, A(π/16) = −1.4268 at the three wavelength ratios used.
**A(κ) is the coefficient of the difference ρ_site − ρ_bond, not of the site-centred floor**
(the two coincide only to the extent that the bond floor is negligible, which §4.5 shows it
is not): at the κ = 0.199 cell that dominates anchor 1's fit, A = −1.4249 (closed form) and
−1.4469 (exact, Prop. 4.3) against a measured difference of −1.4469 and a measured *site*
coefficient of −1.3936.
(Numerically, D(κ) is a cancellation of terms of size κ⁻³, so the float64 closed form is
noise-limited below κ ≈ 1e-4; the archived `A_long_wavelength_limit` = −1.4999999974 is read
off at κ = 1e-4, and the analytic value is exactly −3/2.)

**ASSUMPTION A4.** The step from (4.5) to (4.6) is an asymptotic expansion (midpoint sums →
integrals, ω = 2 sin(k/2) → k, β_edge = a/2 − a²/8R → a/2). Its remainder is **not bounded
here**; it is *measured* per cell (`rel_err_continuum`) to be ≤ **3.6 %** over the nine
(L, λ) cells — 3.608e-02 in the worst cell (L = 8, κ = 0.785) and 8.36e-04 in the best
(L = 32, κ = 0.209). At fixed κ it falls by a factor ≈ 4 per doubling of L (3.6 % → 0.98 %
→ 0.23 % at κ ≈ 0.79; 1.5 % → 0.38 % → 0.084 % at κ ≈ 0.21), i.e. the *observed* decay over
this three-point window is ≈ 1/L², faster than the O(1/L) the expansion formally leaves —
an observation on three values of L, not a bound. Do not quote (4.6) as a theorem with a
controlled error; quote (4.5), which is exact. And do not quote the 0.1 %
(`max_rel_err_discrete`) figure here: that is the accuracy of the *discrete* form, with the
(1 − ρ_bond) factor dropped but the lattice sums kept exact, and it is 43× smaller than the
error of (4.6).

for to 4 % at L = 8 and 14 % at L = 32 at the dominant wavelength, and no better.** This
upgrades anchor 5 from "measured to be a choice" to "the dominant part of it is derived to
be the trapezoid rule's placement error", not to "derived to 0.1 %".

and −1.4180 (L = 32) — L-independent to 0.3 %. So at fixed wavelength ratio the residual
falls as **L^{−2.00}**, not L^{−2.27}.

**(iii) The fitted exponent −2.27 is not the asymptotic rate.** Anchor 1's exponent comes
from a fit of max_ratios r(L) over L = 8…48 at N = 2400. Two contaminations are visible in
the data and are named here as the explanation, not proved to be the whole of it:

* the reported wavelengths are not exactly ratio × L — `squeeze_modes` picks the nearest
  odd Dirichlet mode, so λ jumps in steps (126.37 serves L = 8 at ratio 16 *and* L = 16 at
  ratio 8), and κ therefore drifts with L, moving A along the steep part of A(κ);
* the bond-centred piece is N-limited at large L: its coefficient is +0.053 at L = 8 and
  +0.058 at L = 16 but +0.170 at L = 32 on the same N = 2400 chain, and the MANIFEST already
  records that at N = 6400 it is 0.052/L² at L = 8, 12, 16. The fall of A_site from −1.364
  (L = 16) to −1.248 (L = 32) is entirely this: the *difference* A_site − A_bond falls only
  from −1.4222 to −1.4180 (0.3 %) over the same octave. At L = 24 and 48 the archived r(L)
  is worse still (at L = 48 the λ = 4L cell even changes sign, ρ = +1.9e-04), which is the
  same N-limitation and which is what steepens the fitted slope.

**Conclusion on the rate: what is proven is an exact identity for the ansatz-to-ansatz
difference, whose leading term is A(κ)/L² with exponent exactly −2. The measured −2.27 is a
fit artefact of the (L, λ, N) grid, and is not the continuum rate.** Consequently:

* the **1/L² extrapolation** r_∞ = −1.9e-04 is the functional form the theory supports, and
  its fitted amplitude A_fit = 1.406 (`summary.json`, `A_fit_inverse_L2`) sits within 1–3 %
  of the derived *difference* coefficient −1.4249 (closed form) / −1.4469 (exact) at the
  dominant wavelength, and within 1 % of the *measured site* coefficient −1.3936 at L = 8
  (the site coefficient then drifts to −1.2478 by L = 32 through the N-limited bond floor).
  So the 1/L² fit is fitting the derived artefact **up to the bond floor**, which is 3.8 %
  of it at L = 8 and 13.6 % at L = 32 and is not derived here;
* the **fitted-power extrapolation** r_∞ = +4.6e-04 has no theoretical support: it
  extrapolates an exponent that the derivation says should be −2 and that the data show to
  be −2 at fixed κ. It should be quoted as a robustness check on the fitting procedure, not
  as an independent continuum estimate.

### 4.5 What is *not* derived here

* **ASSUMPTION A5 (the absolute rate).** §4 computes the *difference* between two
  discretisations of the same continuum object. It does not prove that either converges to
  the continuum value, i.e. that r_∞ = 0. Cor. 3.1 says the continuum residual is zero and
  the data say the lattice residual is falling like 1/L², but "the lattice residual
  converges to the continuum residual" is an assumption (a statement about the convergence
  of the ball's exact modular Hamiltonian and of the lattice covariance to their continuum
  limits, uniformly enough on the variation family). It is not proved here.
* **Consequence for the site floor (the point a reader is most likely to over-read).** Because
  the bond floor is not derived, neither is the site floor on its own: §4 derives the
  *difference*. Numerically the unaccounted fraction is |A_bond/A_site| = 3.8 % (L = 8),
  4.2 % (L = 16), 13.6 % (L = 32) at λ = 16 L and up to 116 % at λ = 4 L, L = 32, N = 2400
  (archived as `rel_err_site_only`, pinned by
  `tests/test_toy_jacobson_continuum.py::test_archived_site_floor_is_not_derived_to_one_part_in_a_thousand`).
  The 0.1 % figure (`max_rel_err_discrete`) belongs to the difference alone; it is **not**
  the accuracy with which the site floor is derived.
* **The bond-centred floor 0.053/L² is observed, not derived.** Prop. 4.1 shows the midpoint
  rule removes the β'' term *exactly*, so the derivation predicts zero at this order and is
  silent about what is left. The remaining O(a²) sources are (a) the Euler–Maclaurin
  boundary term of Σ_i β_i p_i²/2 against ∫β π²/2, (b) the lattice dispersion
  ω = k(1 − k²/24 + …), (c) the O(a²) correction to the bond energy itself,
  (Δφ)² = a²[φ'(x_m)² + (a²/12)φ'φ''' + …], and (d) the finite-N (Dirichlet-wall) error.
  The measured bond coefficient is only weakly wavelength-dependent (+0.049, +0.052, +0.053
  at L = 8 across κ = 0.79, 0.40, 0.20), which argues against (b)/(c) — both of which carry
  explicit κ² — and points at a boundary term, but this is a plausibility argument and is
  left as an open problem.
* **Anchor 6's failure of the bond ansatz is untouched.** The MANIFEST records that the
  bond-centred form fails the pure-dispersion test ∂ρ/∂c₃ = 6 ∂ρ/∂c₂ (it gives 1.9–2.2
  instead of 6). §4 concerns only the nearest-neighbour massless chain, where Prop. 4.1's
  straddling-bond term is special (the midpoint of a nearest-neighbour straddling bond lies
  exactly on ∂B). With longer-range couplings that coincidence fails, which is consistent
  with — but is not a derivation of — the bond ansatz's range-dependent artefact.

## 5. The certified numerics

Two steps above are computational. Both are implemented in
`vacuum/geometry/jacobson_continuum.py` — **in mpmath, with no lattice code** — regenerated
by `papers/toy-jacobson/notebook_continuum.py` into `data/H_continuum_check.json` and pinned
by `tests/test_toy_jacobson_continuum.py` (20 tests, 7 s).

### 5.1 The continuum first law with the exact CHM modular Hamiltonian (H1)

**The construction.** By Theorem 2.1 the modular flow of A is z ↦ z + 2πt, so the
**one-particle** modular generator on L²(−R, R) is the self-adjoint differential operator

  δ_β = −2πi ( β(x) d/dx + β'(x)/2 ),  (5.1)

whose generalized eigenfunctions and spectrum are

  ψ_s(x) = β(x)^{−1/2} e^{i s z(x)} / √(2π),  δ_β ψ_s = 2π s ψ_s,  s ∈ ℝ,  (5.2)

as one checks in two lines: β ψ_s′ = −½β^{−1/2}β′e^{isz} + i s β^{−1/2}e^{isz} and the first
term is cancelled by ½β′ψ_s. The ψ_s are δ-normalized in L²(dx) because dx = β dz. Vacuum
thermality at modular temperature 1/(2π) then fixes the symplectic eigenvalue at modular
frequency s,

  ν(s) = n(s) + ½ = 1/(e^{2πs} − 1) + ½ = coth(πs)/2,  (5.3)

and the bosonic entropy weight of (1.a) is

  g(ν(s)) = ln((ν + ½)/(ν − ½)) = 2π s **exactly**.  (5.4)

(5.4) is the whole first law in one line: the weight with which a mode enters δS is its
modular energy.

**The check.** Take the normalized Gaussian modular wave packet
Φ(z) = (πσ²)^{−1/4} e^{−(z−z₀)²/2σ²} e^{i s₀ z}, whose modular-frequency content is
|φ(s)|² = (σ/√π) e^{−σ²(s−s₀)²}, and the position-space packet
χ(x) = Φ(z(x))/√β(x) (normalized: ∫|χ|²dx = ∫|Φ|²dz = 1). The first-order squeeze variation
along χ populates the modular modes with that content, and the two sides of the first law are

  δS  = ∫₀^∞ g(ν(s)) |φ(s)|² ds   (entropy route: (5.3) then (5.4), computed numerically,
                                   **not** simplified to 2πs by hand),
  δ⟨K⟩ = ⟨χ| −2πi ( w d/dx + w′/2 ) |χ⟩  (CHM route: a quadrature over x ∈ (−R, R) in which
                                   the weight w appears explicitly).

For w = β they must agree. Measured at dps = 40 (`H1_continuum_first_law`):

| packet (z₀, σ, s₀) | δS | \|δS − δ⟨K⟩\|/\|δS\| |
|---|---|---|
| (0, 2, 4) | 25.132741228718345 ( = 2π·4) | **4.3e-32** |
| (1.5, 2, 4) | 25.132741228718345 | **4.3e-32** |
| (0, 1, 8) | 50.265482457436690 ( = 2π·8) | **4.3e-32** |

far inside the 1e-12 the proof step needs (6.0e-27 at dps = 25, the setting the test uses).
The residual defect is exactly the unphysical s < 0 tail of the Gaussian
(`neg_s_tail` = 1.085e-30, the same number as `abs_defect`), i.e. the check is limited by
the packet's truncation and not by the identity. Auxiliary certificates in the same run:
‖χ‖ = ‖φ‖ = 1 to 1e-20; Im δ⟨K⟩ ≤ 1.5e-47 (the generator is self-adjoint, so δ⟨K⟩ must be
real); and (5.4) verified directly at s = 0.25, 1, 4, 12 to ≤ 3.4e-52.

**Mutation tests (this is what gives the check teeth).**

| weight | rel. defect |
|---|---|
| CHM β | 4.3e-32 |
| 1.001·β | **1.0e-03** (exactly \|1 − 1.001\|: the identity is linear in w) |
| 0.995·β | **5.0e-03** |
| β(1 + 0.05 x²) — still ≥ 0, still vanishing at ±R | **1.37e-02** |

**What this certifies, and what it does not.** It certifies that the position-space operator
built from the CHM parabola with the CHM normalisation 2π reproduces, on an explicit
Gaussian squeeze, exactly the entropy weight of the vacuum's modular spectrum — i.e. that
steps §1 and §2 fit together with no factor astray, to 32 digits, and that an error of one
part in 10³ in the weight would be visible. It does **not** reprove Theorem 2.1: the modular
spectrum (5.3) is *input*, taken from the cited theorem. It is a consistency certificate of
the continuum formulas, not an independent derivation of CHM.

### 5.2 The conformal-Killing shape test (H2)

By (1.b), a diffeomorphism x ↦ x + ε ξ(x) with ξ supported strictly inside A is implemented
by a unitary of 𝒜(A), so δS = 0 and hence δ⟨K_A⟩ = 0. In a c = 1 chiral theory the vacuum
energy density responds to such a diffeomorphism only through the anomaly,
δ⟨T₀₀(x)⟩ = −(c/24π) ξ‴(x) (⟨T₀₀⟩_vac = 0 kills the inhomogeneous terms), so the first law
requires

  ∫_A w(x) ξ‴(x) dx = 0 for every such ξ  ⟺  w‴ ≡ 0 on A,  (5.5)

which is the conformal Killing condition: the weight must be a quadratic polynomial.
`conformal_killing_defect` evaluates the left side on a C^∞ bump deliberately placed **off
centre** (support (0, R/2)), so that the test is not blinded by parity. Measured (dps = 40):

| weight | rel. defect |
|---|---|
| CHM β | 3.2e-47 |
| 1.001·β | 3.2e-47 — **blind by construction**, the functional is linear in w |
| β + 0.1 x³ | **2.59e-04** |
| β + 0.1 x⁴ | **2.61e-04** |
| β cosh x | **6.0e-04** (in the test file) |

The two checks are complementary: §5.1 fixes the normalisation and is evaluated on one
packet; §5.2 fixes the shape (w‴ = 0) for *every* localized ξ and is blind to the
normalisation. Together they pin β up to the two constants fixed by β(±R) = 0.

### 5.3 The O(a²) prediction (H3)

`bond_split_weight_coefficient` / `bond_split_discretization_prediction` implement (4.6) and
(4.5). The certificate is the last column pair of §4.4: the exact identity (4.5) reproduces
the lattice to **6.3e-12**; dropping the (1 − ρ_bond) factor costs **8.3e-04**; taking the
L → ∞ limit (4.6) costs **3.6e-02** at worst (L = 8, λ = 4L) and **8.4e-04** at best
(L = 32, λ = 16L); at L = 32 the three wavelengths give 2.3e-03, 9.3e-04, 8.4e-04. These
three accuracies belong to three *different* approximations and are archived under three
different keys (`max_rel_err_exact`, `max_rel_err_discrete`, `max_rel_err_continuum` /
`min_rel_err_continuum`); `test_the_closed_form_remainder_is_not_the_discrete_one` asserts
that the last is ≥ 30× the middle one, so the two cannot be quoted for each other.

## 6. Summary: the assumptions, listed

| # | assumption | where it enters | how it could fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1–R3 | trace-norm differentiability of ρ(ε), positivity, absolute convergence of Tr[δρ K] | Prop. 1.1 | in QFT they fail as stated; the relative-entropy form of §1 is what is used |
| — | **Theorem 2.1** (BW / Hislop–Longo / CHM) | §2.1, and the modular spectrum (5.3) used by the H1 certificate | cited, not reproved |
| — | the algebra is the **current/derivative** one, not the φ-algebra with its zero mode | §2.2; guaranteed for anchor 1 by the exact parity Lemma 2.2 | fails for reflection-even variations, where r = O(1) as measured |
| A1 | the massless chain's long-wavelength limit is the c = 1 free boson with v = 1 | §2.3 | standard |
| A2 | the PSD bond split is a representative of T₀₀ up to a discrete divergence | §2.3, §4 | the O(a²) ambiguity is exactly what §4 computes |
| A3 | the ball's boundaries sit at ±L/2 (midway between edge site and neighbour) | (0.3), Prop. 4.1 | a different placement changes β_edge at O(a) and changes A(κ) |
| (3.2) | the variation family is complete enough to determine the weight | Cor. 3.2, §3.3 | **known to fail** for the family used (rank 5–6 per ball); hence "parsimony, not existence" |
| **A4** | the remainder in (4.6) is O(1/L) | §4.3 | measured ≤ 3.6 %, not bounded |
| **A5** | the lattice residual converges to the continuum residual (r_∞ = 0) | §4.5 | not proved; §4 proves only the *difference* between two discretisations |

**In one sentence.** The continuum statement is an identity with no limit in it (Cor. 3.1);
the lattice number is a discretisation error whose dominant part — the trapezoid-versus-
midpoint placement of the CHM weight on the bonds — is an exact closed form for the
*difference between the two ansätze* (Prop. 4.3, certified to 6.3e-12), whose continuum
limit is A(κ)/L² with A(0⁺) = −3/2, so the rate is L^{−2} at fixed wavelength ratio, the fitted −2.27 is an artefact of the (L, λ, N) grid, the
site-centred floor is thereby accounted for only up to the still-underived bond floor
(3.8 %–13.6 % of it at the dominant wavelength), and "r_∞ = 0" itself remains an assumption
(A5) rather than a theorem.
